# Anti-slop contract

- No default card grids, pill labels, gradients, blobs, stock icon rows, faux dashboards, or ornamental rules unless the brief or Design DNA proves they belong.
- No arbitrary color, font, spacing, radius, shadow, or alignment changes. Reuse named roles and anchors.
- Do not fill empty space merely to make a slide look busy. Whitespace, cropping, and omission are design decisions.
- Prefer a few strongly ordered elements to many equally weighted ones. Each element must clarify the message, establish structure, or be removed.
- Keep copy factual and concise; never invent facts, attribution, or imagery.

## Information-bearing multi-view structures

Paired, stacked, nested, and bridged diagrams are permitted when they explain a real relationship that one view cannot: comparison, dependency, containment, handoff, or exchange. They are not faux dashboards or default card grids when each view answers a distinct reader question and labels the relationship to the others.

- Keep only views, boundaries, connectors, and repeated elements that carry information. Remove ornamental containers, duplicate labels, and symmetry added only to fill space.
- Preserve a single message and deliberate reading path across views. Use shared alignment, type, color, and connector semantics from the Design DNA instead of inventing a visual treatment for each view.
- Repetition is valid when it establishes a meaningful comparison, stable system boundary, or repeated step; variation is valid when the relationship changes. Do not require either novelty or a fixed number of views.
