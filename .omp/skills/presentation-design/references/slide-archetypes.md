# Slide archetypes

Choose an archetype from the message and the established Design DNA, not from a generic component library. An archetype controls the reading path; it is not a reason to add decoration.

- **Title or section**: establish the topic or transition with one dominant read and enough space to reset attention.
- **Assertion**: lead with one claim, then place only the evidence needed to support it.
- **Comparison**: give alternatives equivalent structure so the difference, not the container treatment, carries the message.
- **Process**: make sequence, direction, and handoff explicit; use one path rather than competing flows.
- **Evidence**: make the chart, quote, number, or image the focal read and retain the context needed to interpret it.
- **Image-led**: let the image carry the subject while type supplies a concise frame; crop intentionally inside its frame.
- **Close**: restate the intended takeaway or action with a clear final hierarchy.

Vary adjacent archetypes when the narrative benefits from a change in rhythm, but preserve the deck’s anchors, type scale, color roles, and density.
